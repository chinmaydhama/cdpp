The short version

I never made one clean career switch. I kept adding layers. Engineering taught me systems. Recykal taught me real ML. ElectricPe taught me the model is not the product. Washington handed me messy data at campus scale. Tempora put me inside AI products. At IQRush, every layer runs at once.

012019 – 2022IIT Dharwad

It started with machines.

In 2019 I walked into IIT Dharwad to study mechanical engineering. My world was powertrains, tolerances and performance curves. At InGene Motorsport I spent a year and a half as a powertrain engineer, where every design change had a physical consequence you could measure. Short stints at RIYFT and OLL showed me how constraints turn into design decisions.

I still open every problem with the same four questions:

  1. What goes in?
  2. What happens inside?
  3. What can we measure?
  4. Where is the bottleneck?

What it taught meHow systems work.

02Aug – Dec 2022Recykal

Then I pointed models at trucks and trash.

Recykal runs recycling logistics, where the margin lives in routes, fuel and timing. My first real data science job was about moving material more cheaply and knowing what was coming before it arrived.

As an intern I worked on four problems: forecasting logistics volumes and prices with LSTMs, route and fuel optimization, customer segmentation with K-means, and Mask R-CNN segmentation of waste images. Five months, four different kinds of model, one question behind all of them: what does the operation need to know before it happens?

  • LSTM forecasting volumes · prices
  • Route & fuel optimization logistics cost
  • K-means segmentation
  • Mask R-CNN waste images

What it taught meModels can optimize real, physical operations.

02

03Jan – May 2023ElectricPe

Where I learned the model is not the product.

At ElectricPe I switched sides of the table. As Associate Product Manager on a B2B platform for EV charging, I wrote PRDs, set acceptance criteria, prioritized with engineers and took features from idea to release. The platform grew toward roughly 30,000 users while I was there. That's the company's number, not mine, but it's the scale my decisions had to work at.

It changed what I ask before I build anything:

  1. Who is this for?
  2. What decision are they trying to make?
  3. What happens when the system is wrong?
  4. What does "done" actually mean?

What it taught meThe model is not the product.

03

04Aug 2023 – Jul 2025GWU → ABM Industries

I came to D.C. for a degree and ended up running data for a campus.

In 2023 I moved to Washington for an MS in Data Science at George Washington University, where I earned a 3.9 GPA and the Global Leaders Fellowship. Three months in, I was also working inside GWU Facilities, the team that keeps 45+ buildings running.

It was a giant, messy, real system: HVAC, electrical, plumbing, thousands of assets, and data split across CMMS, finance, building systems and meters. I connected them, built automated QA checks that flag bad asset and work-order records before they reach anyone's report, and forecast building energy at 30-minute resolution with 25–30% lower error than the approach the team used before.

That work is how I got to ABM Industries, the facilities operator on the same campus. Same buildings, bigger scope: 5M+ square feet, 1,200+ work orders a month, and pipelines from CMMS, ERP and BMS into automated KPI dashboards.

05

06Aug 2025 → nowIQRush.ai

Now I measure how AI sees the world.

I joined IQRush.ai as employee #4 and I'm now a Senior Data Scientist. We answer a hard question: why does an AI answer mention one brand, page or source and not another?

An LLM has no ranking you can read. It retrieves, generates, cites, and changes its mind between runs. So I build the measurement first, then the models, then the product on top, and I own it end to end on Azure. Most of the hard work is the last 10%: making a number stable, meaningful and something a customer can act on.

  • #4employee, joined Aug 2025
  • 62→12%run-to-run score instability after removing hard cutoffshow measured ↗
  • ~10client deployments I work on directly

Step 01 of 06

Customer

It usually starts with a sentence like "we're invisible in ChatGPT." I sit in those conversations, working forward-deployed with around 10 clients.

  • Ranking

    Recommendations that act

    Calibrated classifiers and ranking models over 100+ engineered features, turned into specific changes a marketer can make, with states that behave sensibly across repeated runs.

  • Evaluation

    LLM evaluation

    Statement extraction, semantic matching and a smooth confidence roll-up in place of hard cutoffs, so scores stay stable enough to trust.

  • Retrieval

    Retrieval emulation

    Estimating which pages and sections an AI system would surface for a query, with every finding linked back to its evidence.

  • Causal

    Did it actually work?

    A/B tests, cohorts and difference-in-differences to show whether a content change moved citations, not just correlated with them.

  • Product

    Content Playground

    An agentic editor that writes, rewrites, gathers evidence and rescores by page, section and statement, with the user in control.

  • Graph

    Co-mention network

    A graph of which brands and sources appear together inside AI answers: competitive intelligence you can't get from backlinks.

What it's teaching meBuild the measurement, the model and the product at the same time.
